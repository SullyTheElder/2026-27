The Situation

The Bulldog Esports Invitational committee asked an AI tool to design the event's network. Before any of it gets near real equipment, your job is to check it. AI tools are good at producing ideas fast. They are not good at knowing what your event needs.

To judge a design, you first need a way to judge how devices get connected. So we start with some math.

Concept: Choosing a Topology

A topology is the way a network's devices are connected. Networks pick one based on reliability, cost, and ease of setup and maintenance.

  • Star: every device connects to a central switch. Cheap, easy to set up and troubleshoot, and a good fit when there are limited resources. The weakness: the switch is a single point of failure.
  • Mesh: every device connects directly to every other device. There are many paths, so it has high fault tolerance, but it needs far more cabling, devices, and management. It suits networks with dedicated support.
  • Hybrid: larger networks often mix star and mesh to balance performance, reliability, and cost.
  • Bus and ring topologies are generally avoided today because they handle failures and growth poorly.

2 Critique the AI's Design about 20 min

Goal: decide what the AI got right, what it got wrong, and what it left out.

What the committee typed

Design a network for the Bulldog Esports Invitational.

What the AI answered (written for this class)

Bulldog Esports Invitational: Network Design

  1. Topology: Full mesh. Every device connects directly to every other device, so the network keeps working no matter what fails.
  2. Segments: One network, 192.168.1.0/24, shared by everyone: players, casters, admins, coaches, referees, staff, and spectators.
  3. Devices: One 24-port switch and one wireless access point.
  4. Security: Protect the Wi-Fi with a strong password.
  5. Players: Players connect by cable for low lag.

What the committee actually needs (from U3L01):

  • 10 players, 3 casters, and 2 admins wired. Players can't drop out mid-match.
  • A local tournament server.
  • 4 coaches, 3 referees, and 10 staff on laptops and tablets.
  • 120 spectators on their phones.
  • Spectators must not be able to reach tournament systems.
  • Volunteers set it up, with limited tech support and a tight budget.

Vocabulary

Topology
The way a network's devices are connected.
Star topology
Every device connects to a central switch.
Mesh topology
Every device connects directly to every other device.
Hybrid topology
A mix of star and mesh used to balance cost, performance, and reliability.
Single point of failure
One part that, if it fails, takes the whole network down.
Fault tolerance
The ability to keep working when something fails.
