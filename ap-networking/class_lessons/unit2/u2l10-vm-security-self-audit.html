What You're Doing Today

Two lessons ago (U2L08) you ran netstat on your own VMs to see which ports and services were listening. Last class (U2L09) you learned four categories of security vulnerability by auditing someone else's router.

Today you're combining both: you'll re-run the same inventory on your own machines, then reason about your own results the way you reasoned about the mock router panel — nothing new to learn, just applying it to a real target: you.

Part 1 — Re-run the Inventory

  1. Log into CyberRange and launch your Windows 7 and Ubuntu VMs.
  2. On Windows 7, open Command Prompt and run: netstat -ano
  3. On Ubuntu, open a terminal and run: netstat -tuln
  4. Record what you find below — same as U2L08, at least 3 listening ports per machine.

Part 2 — Audit Yourself

Remember the four vulnerability categories from U2L09: default configurations, outdated firmware, unmonitored/unknown devices, and insecure IoT devices sharing a network. Your VM isn't a router, but the same reasoning applies to any machine on a network.

Answer the following using your own results from Part 1:

  1. Pick one listening port/service you found. If you didn't already know what it was, look it up in one sentence — what does it do?
  2. Is there anything in your results you wouldn't expect a typical user's machine to be running? If so, what, and why might an unexpected open service be a risk (think back to the vulnerability categories)?
  3. If this VM were a real computer on a home network, would you consider any of what you found something worth investigating further? Why or why not?
