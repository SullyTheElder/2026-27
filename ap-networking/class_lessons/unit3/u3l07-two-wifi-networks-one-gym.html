The Situation

About 120 spectators will walk into the gym for the Bulldog Esports Invitational, and every one of them will ask for the Wi-Fi password. Meanwhile, staff, coaches, and referees use tablets that must reach the tournament systems.

The committee has two Wi-Fi designs on the table. Your job is to work out what each one allows, before anyone picks.

Concept: Wireless Segmentation

Devices and users should be grouped by how sensitive they are, what access they need, and what traffic they create. Splitting guests, smart (IoT) devices, user workstations, and internal services reduces congestion and makes it harder for an attacker to move sideways from one device to another.

On wireless, you do this with multiple SSIDs. An SSID is a Wi-Fi network's name. Use separate SSIDs when different groups need separate wireless access, such as staff and guests. Each SSID can be assigned its own VLAN or subnet, which gives you both traffic isolation and access control.

Notice the careful wording: a separate subnet is the start of the protection. Something still has to stop guest traffic from crossing into the internal network. You'll set that up tomorrow.

Vocabulary

SSID
The name of a Wi-Fi network.
Guest network
A separate Wi-Fi network for visitors, meant to give internet access without access to internal resources.
Internal network
The private network for staff and the event's own systems.
VLAN
A way to keep groups of devices on separate logical networks even when they share hardware.
Traffic isolation
Keeping one group's traffic away from another group's devices.
Access control
Rules that decide who can reach what.
Lateral movement
An attacker moving from one compromised device to others on the same network.
