Concept: Threats and Vulnerabilities in a Segmented LAN

Networks with many kinds of users and devices commonly face unauthorized access, unverified device connections, and data exposure. These risks are why access controls matter: they protect confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

  • No separation (finding 4 and 6): when user groups, devices, and resources aren't properly separated, unauthorized users can reach administrative systems and confidential data, and heavy use by one group can interfere with shared resources and everyone's productivity.
  • Weak wireless security (finding 2): simple passwords, outdated encryption like WEP, or no encryption at all let unauthorized users reach sensitive data.
  • Unknown devices (finding 1): an unverified device on the network may intercept traffic, spread malware, or bypass internal security controls.
  • Weak firewall rules (finding 3): an adversary can move laterally, from one system or segment to many, when firewall rules are weak or misconfigured.
  • Data exposure (finding 5): sensitive information is readable by people who shouldn't see it.

Notice the viewpoint: you were the administrator checking your own network. That's how real networks stay safe: find the weak spots first. Next class, you'll choose the controls that fix them.

Vocabulary

Vulnerability
A weakness that could be used to cause harm.
Threat
Something that could take advantage of a weakness.
Unauthorized access
Someone reaching systems or data they have no right to.
Confidentiality, integrity, availability
Keeping data private, keeping it unaltered, and keeping it available when needed.
Lateral movement
An attacker moving from one compromised device to others on the same network or other segments.
Unverified device
A device connected to the network that no one has checked or approved.
Segmentation
Separating devices and users into groups so problems and access stay contained.
