Concept: Security Controls for a Segmented LAN

Segmented networks use security controls to reduce the impact of unauthorized access. Good controls follow the principle of least privilege: users and devices get only the minimum access they need to do their jobs.

  • Limit unauthorized access: isolated guest wireless networks, distinct address ranges (DHCP pools) for device groups, and physically separate network hardware.
  • Limit the impact of weak wireless security: a unique SSID, a strong password, and WPA2/WPA3 encryption.
  • Limit unknown or unverified devices: MAC address filtering (allow only approved devices), shutting down unused ports, and DHCP reservations that give addresses only to known devices.
  • Limit lateral movement: allow only essential services (like printing) between segments, and use deny-by-default firewall policies that block traffic between subnets.

Notice the trade-off you faced: stronger separation usually costs more money, setup, or upkeep. Choosing appropriate controls means matching them to the real risk and the resources you have. MAC filtering and firewall policies get a deeper look in the next topic.

Vocabulary

Security control
A measure that reduces the chance or impact of a threat.
Principle of least privilege
Giving users and devices only the minimum access needed to do their tasks.
Deny by default
A policy that blocks everything unless a rule specifically allows it.
MAC address filtering
Allowing only approved devices to connect, based on each device's hardware address.
DHCP reservation
A setting that always gives the same address to a specific, known device.
Guest network
A separate network for visitors, kept away from internal systems.
